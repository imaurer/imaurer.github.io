Description

Ron Laneve runs Bell Falls Search and has known Ian since 1999, when Ian became his first executive-search placement. Ian walks through the career: a Commodore 64 with a BASIC interpreter, computer engineering at Syracuse, and a Lockheed Martin internship that paid for his master's degree. Ian chose the web-documentation group over the higher-status sonar department because the web group held the hardest problems. He then moved to Cleveland, spent nine months at a big consulting firm, tried independent consulting, and in April 2004 joined the small firm where he met Brad, his boss ever since. That firm grew through mergers into a thousand-person agency, and Ian shipped e-commerce sites for Sterling Jewelers, Tractor Supply, and HH Gregg before joining GenomOncology.

Ian retells the GenomOncology founding. Papers in 2011 showed genomic analysis could guide cancer treatment, but the analysis took six months while sequencing costs fell faster than Moore's Law. The joke was a thousand-dollar genome and a hundred-thousand-dollar analysis. The company got a molecular pathology report down to fifteen minutes, then kept pulling the next problem off the client's list. The knowledge base now curates about 13,000 clinical trials at the biomarker level, with expert-system rules over disease, biomarkers, and prior therapy, plus a real-world evidence database for comparing treatment outcomes.

Ian gives students the same advice he followed. Take on substantial projects, because nothing meaningful comes out of a twenty-hour effort. Practice deep work, design a maker's schedule, and build a rare combination of skills. For experienced candidates, he names his pet peeve: resumes that list technologies or team memberships without saying what the person actually did.

On AI, Ian argues both camps win. Generative models scale up on GPUs while symbolic expert systems supply codified knowledge, and the two combine like system-one and system-two thinking. He calls generative AI a powerful new form of automation rather than true intelligence, and he describes building the GenomOncology assistant: a chatbot grounded in the curated knowledge base with an accountable human expert in the loop.
